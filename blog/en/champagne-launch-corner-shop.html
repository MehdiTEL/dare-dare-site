If we were a champagne house: we'd launch our cuvée in a late-night corner shop

Agence Dare-Dare · October 2026 · 6 min read

A shop basket on the floor between the aisles, a guest in red mules

Photo: via Pinterest.

The short answer If we were launching a champagne cuvée tomorrow, we would open a fake late-night corner shop: a neon sign, shelves stocked with bottles, a till where guests pay in tokens, branded mesh bags, a crisps and caviar tasting, a DJ until late and a venue designed for Reels. Hijacking an everyday place is the most direct way to make luxury fun, approachable and unforgettable.

A champagne launch is often a hushed salon, a row of flutes and a talk about the blend. Elegant, yes. Memorable, not always.

If we were a champagne house, we would do the opposite: open a late-night corner shop. The one down the road, open when everything else is shut, with its buzzing neon and overstuffed shelves. Except here, everything is chic, and everything sparkles. Here's our plan.

01

The late-night corner shop

Venue Concept Immersion
Model in a graphic black-and-white outfit at the entrance of a corner shop, among bananas and fruit crates

Photo: via Pinterest.

We turn a boutique, a former shop or a raw space into a late-night corner shop more real than the real thing: a neon sign with the cuvée's name, a metal shutter half raised, tiled floors, humming glass-door fridges. Guests push the door and hear the little bell.

Everything feels familiar, yet nothing is. That twist gets a smile from the doorway, and gets the phones out.

02

Shelves of bottles

Set design Merchandising Design
Model in a satin dress leaning on a shopping trolley full of bottles, in front of the drinks aisle

Photo: @kuz___alexa, via Pinterest.

On the shelves, the cuvée is everywhere: neatly faced up, stacked in a pyramid on the end display, in the fridges next to the fizzy drinks. Price tags are swapped for tasting notes, and special offers for puns.

We fill the gaps with fake everyday products in the house colours: packets of crisps, tins, chewing gum. Every shelf becomes a little visual joke, and a showcase for the bottle.

03

The till and the branded bags

Ritual Packaging Signature
Clear bag full of groceries, a bottle of wine, a baguette and d'Alba sunscreens

Photo: d'Alba, via Pinterest.

At the door, every guest is handed tokens. They do their shopping, head to the till, the cashier scans, and the receipt prints with a little note from the house. Nobody really pays, everybody plays along.

Purchases go home in branded mesh bags, the kind you keep and take to the market. An ordinary object turned collector's item: exactly what we want to see doing the rounds after the night.

04

Crisps and caviar

Tasting Pairings Food
Pyramid of crisps topped with cream and caviar on a bed of ham

Photo: Tsar Nicoulai Caviar, via Pinterest.

At the counter, a tasting bar that leans into the contrast: salted crisps and caviar, chips and champagne, triangle sandwiches reworked by a chef. Salt and crunch go beautifully with bubbles, and this is the chance to prove it.

A cellar master or sommelier steps behind the counter to tell the cuvée's story in a few words, no jargon. Guests taste, compare and laugh. And water and food stay within easy reach all night.

05

Content designed for Reels

Content Social media Virality
Young woman in dark glasses with a green Prada bag in an orange shop basket, at a greengrocer's

Photo: @best.dressed, via Pinterest.

Hijacking an everyday place works really well on Reels right now: the contrast between a setting everyone knows and a luxury product lands in a second, with no sound and no caption. A late-night corner shop is a backdrop recognised everywhere.

So we design the venue like a set: the neon glow, the moment at the till, the bag dropping onto the counter, a spoonful of caviar on a crisp. We prepare a shot list for invited creators, and each one leaves with videos in line with your campaign.

06

Open till late

Party DJ Nightlife

At a set time, the lights go down, the DJ sets up behind the till and the shop turns into a party. The fridges stay lit, the neon flickers, and the night carries on between the aisles.

A late-night shop, by definition, closes late. This is the moment the cuvée becomes a party memory, the one everyone talks about the next day.

And the gifting?

We send guests and press a branded mesh bag, packed like a midnight shop run: a bottle of the cuvée, a packet of crisps in the house colours, a small tin of caviar and the till receipt, with a hand-written note. The chicest shopping of the year, and a gift made to be unboxed on camera.

Frequently asked questions

How do you organise a wine or champagne launch event?

Start from the cuvée's story and how you want people to drink it, choose a concept and a venue that bring it to life, plan a proper tasting with pairings, invite press, wine merchants and content creators, and design every space to produce images. And don't forget water, food and ways home for guests.

Why launch a luxury product in an everyday venue?

Because contrast surprises and gets shared: a premium product in a familiar setting is understood instantly, raises a smile and makes the brand feel closer without cheapening it. It's also great ground for content, since everyone recognises the place.

What format works for a press and influencer launch?

Often two parts: a calmer slot for the press, with a tasting and time with the house, then a livelier evening for content creators and guests. A venue designed for filming and a shot list prepared in advance make all the difference.

A cuvée is craftsmanship. Our job is to turn it into an experience people live, share and film, from the neon sign to the DJ's last track.
