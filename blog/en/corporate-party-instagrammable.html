How to throw a brand event guests actually post about

Agence Dare-Dare · October 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer Guests post a party when it gives them a great picture of themselves. You need a concept recognisable in one photo, a signature photo moment that is nothing like a wall of logos, lighting designed for phones, a surprise timed to be filmed, details made to be shared and a content plan that never spoils the experience. Nobody posts out of obligation: people post because it looks good.

You can tell a brand event went well by looking at phones the next morning. Or not. Too often, the only images going round are the comms team's, taken in front of a branded backdrop under meeting-room lighting.

The good news: making a party shareable isn't down to luck. It comes from decisions made upstream, from the concept to the angle of the lights. Here are the six levers we pull every time.

01

A concept that reads in one image

Concept Theme Twist
Party in a booked-out launderette, a crowd between the machines under a disco ball

A concept you recognise in one image: the launderette. Photo: via Pinterest.

A party people post is first an idea you get at a glance, no caption needed. The most effective approach: hijack an everyday place. A fashion brand in the launderette, jewellery sold like pastries in the bakery, a sports launch at the tennis club.

The twist makes people smile, and what makes people smile gets shared. A vague theme like "elegance" or "all-white party" gives nobody a story to tell. A precise world, with its codes and its objects, gives every guest their own photo.

02

A signature photo moment

Photo moment Set design Signature
Giant washing machine photocall: a visitor posing inside the porthole

A photocall people want to try. Bubble Planet. Photo: via Pinterest.

Nobody wants to be seen in front of a vinyl wall of logos. A great photo moment is a set within the set: a counter, a booth, an installation, something people want to step into and that extends the concept.

The brand is there, but with style: on an object, a discreet neon, a detail you spot when you zoom in. Guests go there on their own, and the photo tells the story of your party rather than your brand guidelines.

03

Lighting designed for phones

Lighting Atmosphere Smartphone
Young woman taking a selfie with a folding phone on a gingham tablecloth, in a bistro covered in retro posters

Dazed editorial with the Samsung Galaxy Z Flip. Photo: via Pinterest.

A dark room feels great in real life and looks terrible on screen. Phones love warm, soft light that falls on faces. They hate harsh neon, saturated lasers and backlighting.

We design the lighting with a lighting designer as if for a film set: well-lit areas where people take photos, shadows where they dance. Everyone looks good, and that's what it takes for everyone to post.

04

A surprise timed to be filmed

Surprise Reveal Timing

A party that gets posted needs a high point: a reveal, a performance, an entrance, a curtain drop. Something nobody sees coming and everyone wants to film.

The secret is timing. Not too early, when the room is still empty, not too late, when batteries are flat. We hint at it just enough for phones to come out, and make it last long enough for a full story.

Party with a DJ poster

Inspiration · via Pinterest.

05

Details made to be shared

Objects Food styling Packaging

The best stories often come from small details: a glass engraved with the guest's name, a menu printed like a till receipt, canapés served in unexpected packaging, a gift that unboxes beautifully on video.

Every object is one more photo opportunity. We design them like film props: consistent with the concept, pretty enough to keep, funny enough to show off.

06

A content plan that spoils nothing

Content Creators Experience
Three friends in colourful glasses posing close together in a shop aisle, flash photo

Photo: via Pinterest.

To guarantee images, we prepare the ground: a few invited content creators, a shot list drawn up in advance, a photographer and videographer who know the running order, a corner designed for filming in peace.

But the guest experience comes first. Nobody has to post, nobody gets interrupted mid-conversation. If the party is truly good, the images come on their own: our plan is simply there so nothing gets missed.

What we avoid

The imposed hashtag plastered everywhere, the dreary branded backdrop, the room plunged into darkness and the string of speeches that kills the party's momentum. A short welcome, yes. Three speeches just as the party gets going, no: that's exactly when phones go back into pockets.

Frequently asked questions

How do you make a brand event instagrammable?

Start from a concept recognisable in one image, plan a photo moment that extends that concept, design the lighting for phones, schedule a surprise worth filming and multiply shareable details. All without ever pushing guests to post.

Should you invite influencers to a brand event?

It depends on the goal. For a launch or a brand event, a few content creators chosen because they fit your world are worth more than a long list.

How do you measure a party's social media impact?

Stick to concrete, verifiable elements: mentions of your account, shared stories, posts by guests and creators, and user-generated content you can reuse with their permission. An honest report beats an inflated figure.

A party guests post about isn't a party that begs for posts. It's a party where every choice, from the concept to the lighting, makes people want to take out their phone at the right moment, then put it away and enjoy themselves.
