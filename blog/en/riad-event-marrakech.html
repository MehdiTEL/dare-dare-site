Hosting an event in a riad in Marrakech: the guide

Agence Dare-Dare · October 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer Hosting an event in a riad in Marrakech means taking over an entire house built around its courtyard. It suits an intimate group, from a handful of guests to a few dozen depending on the venue, for a candlelit dinner around the fountain, a seminar or a private party. The essentials: plan access through the medina, check the alcohol policy, choose spring or autumn and book early.

Behind an unassuming door, at the end of a medina alley, the noise of the city fades away. A courtyard planted with orange trees, a fountain, zellige tiles, lounges open to the sky: a riad is an invitation to entertain differently.

For a seminar, a private party or a launch, hiring a riad offers an intimacy no large hotel can match. But the medina has its own rules, and a riad isn't organised like a function room. Here is what we have learnt on the ground, with our network of trusted partners in Morocco.

01

Why choose a riad

Intimacy Courtyard Hospitality

A riad is built around its courtyard: a house that looks inwards and shelters its guests from the outside world. For an event, that is a rare luxury. Your guests have the place to themselves, somewhere that feels like a home rather than a hotel.

The architecture does the rest: arches, carved wood, chiselled plaster, rooftop terraces. Above all, there is the Moroccan art of hospitality, which starts with mint tea and never really stops. The setting is already there; we simply bring it to life.

02

Hiring a whole riad: how it works

Exclusive hire Capacity Accommodation

There are two main options: riad hotels, which can be booked in their entirety for your stay, and private riads, rented like a house. Either way, exclusive hire means the venue is reserved for your group alone.

Capacities vary a great deal from one riad to another, depending on the number of rooms and the size of the courtyard. A riad is especially well suited to groups of around ten to a few dozen people. For larger groups, we bring together several neighbouring riads, or keep the riad for one evening and house guests elsewhere.

03

Dinner in the courtyard

Dinner Candles Live music

This is the heart of the event. Tables are laid around the fountain, candles and lanterns are lit, petals float in the pool. Overhead, the Marrakech sky serves as the ceiling.

At the table, Moroccan sharing dishes or a more contemporary menu, as you prefer, from the riad's own kitchen or a trusted caterer. Gnawa musicians, a string quartet or a DJ: we tailor the mood to the moment. One thing to check early on: some riads do not serve alcohol. We confirm this with each venue before suggesting it to you.

04

The medina: access and logistics

Medina Porters Welcome

Cars cannot get through the medina's alleys. Guests therefore arrive on foot from one of the city gates or an accessible square. We organise the welcome there: hosts, lanterns and guides who walk each group to the riad.

Equipment, furniture, sound, flowers and luggage all travel by porter and handcart. That calls for precise scheduling and extra set-up time. This is where our local network makes all the difference.

05

Pairing it with Agafay or the Palmeraie

Agafay Palmeraie Evening

A riad lends itself perfectly to a programme over several days. Guests stay there, dine there and gather there between engagements. For a more spectacular evening, we head out to the Agafay desert or an estate in the Palmeraie.

The contrast works beautifully: the intimacy of the courtyard one night, the vastness of the desert the next. In between, a visit to the Majorelle Garden or a stroll through the souks. We coordinate the transfers so everything flows effortlessly.

06

Timing: seasons and booking

Seasons Ramadan Planning ahead

Spring and autumn offer ideal conditions for dining outdoors in a courtyard or on a terrace. Summer is very hot, and winter evenings can be chilly: plan for braziers and throws.

Remember the Ramadan dates too, which shift every year and change the rhythm of the city. Finally, the most beautiful riads are booked early, especially in high season. The sooner you plan, the wider the choice.

Our checklist before you start

Decide on your guest numbers and whether they will stay overnight. Ask whether the riad can be hired exclusively and what that includes. Check the venue's alcohol policy. Find the nearest medina gate and plan how guests will be welcomed. Allow for equipment to be carried in by porters. Check the Ramadan dates. And rely on a local team that knows the riads, the suppliers and the permits.

Frequently asked questions

How many guests can a riad accommodate?

It depends entirely on the riad: number of rooms, courtyard size, terraces. Most suit groups of around ten to a few dozen people. We will point you to the right venue for your group.

Can you hire an entire riad?

Yes, many riad hotels and private riads can be hired exclusively, for one evening or a whole stay. Terms vary from venue to venue, and we check them for you.

Can alcohol be served in a riad?

It depends on the venue. Some riads do not serve alcohol, others allow it. We check this with each riad before suggesting it to you.

What is the best season for an event in a riad?

Spring and autumn, for mild days and pleasant evenings in the courtyard. Check the Ramadan dates too, as they shift every year.

How far ahead should you book, and what budget should you plan?

As early as possible, as the finest riads go quickly. The budget depends on the venue, the length of stay, guest numbers and services: we send you a quote detailed line by line.
