If we were a shoe brand: we'd launch our collection at a cobbler's

Agence Dare-Dare · October 2026 · 6 min read · Updated 24 October 2026

The short answer If we were launching a shoe collection tomorrow, we would book out a cobbler's workshop: the styles displayed on wooden lasts and repair shelves, an invitation in the form of a repair ticket, a personalisation workshop (initials, laces, patina), a venue designed for Reels and a party with a shoe shine bar and a DJ. The cobbler's is where shoes get looked after: the perfect setting to tell the story of craftsmanship.

A shoe launch is often a white showroom, pairs lined up on plinths and a buffet. Nice, but everyone has seen it before.

If we were a shoe brand, we'd go where shoes really live: the cobbler's. The smell of leather, wooden lasts, pinned-up tickets, the stitching machine, the polish. Everything already speaks of craft. All we'd have to do is set our collection down. Here's our plan.

01

The private cobbler's workshop

Venue Craft Immersion
Shopfront of a cobbler’s shop

Photo: via Pinterest.

We book out a neighbourhood cobbler's, the kind that has kept its wooden counter, its tools hung on the wall and its hand-painted sign. The cobbler stays at the workbench, hard at work, as guests walk in.

The venue does most of the work: authentic, warm, full of detail. We simply dress it in the brand's colours, with leather aprons, hand-drawn signage and workshop lighting.

02

The collection on lasts and shelves

Set design Collection Product
Cobbler’s tools and a wooden last

Photo: via Pinterest.

The new styles take the place of pairs waiting to be mended: slipped onto wooden lasts, lined up on the shelves with their tags, set on the workbench next to the awls and hammers.

Every pair seems to be waiting for its owner. Guests don't look at a shop window: they browse the shelves as if they'd come to collect their own shoes.

03

The repair ticket invitation

Invitation Stationery Teasing
Selected leathers

Photo: via Pinterest.

The invitation arrives as a numbered cobbler's ticket with a tear-off stub: the guest's name, "work required: discover the new collection", and a collection date.

On the day, everyone hands their ticket over the counter to get in. A small object people keep, photograph, and that sets the tone the moment it lands on the doormat.

04

The personalisation workshop

Workshop Personalisation Creators
Handmade shoemaking

Photo: via Pinterest.

Dimitri Gomez, master bootmaker, in his workshop

Photo: Dimitri Gomez, master bootmaker · via Pinterest.

Around the workbench, artisans personalise pairs on the spot: embossed initials, a choice of laces, hand-applied patina, edge colour. Guests pick, watch the work being done and leave with a one-of-a-kind pair.

It's the best way to tell the craft story: no more talk of leather and finishes, just the work on show. And for content creators, those precise gestures are a joy to film.

05

Content designed for Reels

Content Social media Virality
Craft report: leatherworking tools and sewing machine

Photo: via Pinterest.

Reels that hijack an everyday place are doing really well right now: the surprise stops the scroll, and a spot everyone knows makes the brand instantly familiar. The cobbler's is a dream backdrop: wooden lasts, a ticket handed over, polish gleaming, close-ups on hands at work.

So we design the venue like a set: warm light, angles planned for filming the workbench, and a ready-made shot list for invited creators. The result: Reels in line with your campaign, filmed in a setting you won't see anywhere else.

06

The shoe shine bar party

Party DJ Shoe shine
Red pumps in an elevator

Photo: via Pinterest.

As night falls, the cobbler's changes gear: an old-school shoe shine stand, raised chair and brass footrests, polishing guests' shoes, a drinks bar on the counter and a DJ behind the workbench.

This is the moment the brand becomes the party everyone talks about, the one where you left with shinier shoes than you arrived in.

And the gifting?

We finish with a workshop-style shoebox: a pair from the collection, a care kit (polish, brush, cloth), a spare pair of laces and the repair ticket stamped "job done". The cobbler's to take home, and a gift made to be unboxed on camera.

Frequently asked questions

How do you organise a shoe collection launch?

Start from the craft and the story of the collection, choose a venue where guests can touch and try the styles rather than just look at them behind glass, invite press and content creators, and design every space to produce images.

Why launch shoes at a cobbler's?

Because the cobbler stands for care, durability and craft: it tells the story of a pair's quality without a speech, it offers an authentic and highly photogenic setting, and it makes on-site personalisation feel natural.

How much does a shoe launch event cost?

It depends on the venue, the guest count, the set design and the activities planned. Agence Dare-Dare puts together a quote detailed line by line from your brief, so you know exactly where every euro goes.
