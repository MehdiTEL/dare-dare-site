How much should you budget for a corporate party in Paris?

Agence Dare-Dare · October 2026 · 8 min read

The short answer In Paris, a corporate party typically costs €150 to €250 excl. VAT per guest for a simple, well-executed format, €250 to €450 excl. VAT for a premium party, and over €450 excl. VAT for a prestige event in an iconic venue. For 150 guests, that means somewhere between €25,000 and €70,000 excl. VAT, all in. Catering and venue alone make up half the budget.

"How much will it cost?" It is the first question in almost every brief, and fair enough. The trouble is that the figures you find online range from one to ten times each other, and you never know what they include.

So here are the ballpark figures we actually see on the ground in Paris, line by line, with a full sample budget. Amounts are indicative, excluding VAT, and vary with the date, the venue and your level of ambition.

Budget per guest by party level

Thinking per guest is the simplest way to frame a budget. Here are the three levels we see most often:

LevelWhat it includesBudget excl. VAT / guest
EssentialFunctional venue, walking dinner, DJ, basic technical set-up€150 to €250
PremiumVenue with character, set design, entertainment, polished welcome€250 to €450
PrestigeIconic venue, seated dinner, artists, bespoke production€450 and up

Guest numbers matter too: below 80 people, the cost per head goes up, because the venue and technical costs are spread over fewer guests.

The cost breakdown, line by line

1. The venue: 20 to 30% of the budget

In Paris, hiring a venue for an evening ranges from around €3,000 excl. VAT for a simple space to over €50,000 excl. VAT for a monument or a palace hotel. Always check what is included: furniture, cloakroom, security, end time, in-house or open catering. A cheaper venue that imposes its own caterer can end up costing more. 2. Catering and drinks: 30 to 40% of the budget

This is the biggest line. Allow around €60 to €130 excl. VAT per person for a walking dinner and €100 to €250 excl. VAT for a seated dinner, plus drinks (open bar, champagne) and serving staff. The walking dinner remains the most popular format: it keeps guests mingling and costs less than a seated dinner.

3. Sound, lighting and video: 10 to 15%

From €3,000 excl. VAT for a PA and ambient lighting to €20,000 excl. VAT and up for a stage, screens and a full control desk. It is the line most often underestimated, and the one that makes the difference between a room and an atmosphere.

4. Entertainment and artists: 5 to 15%

A DJ usually costs between €800 and €5,000 excl. VAT depending on their profile. Photobooth, performers, live band or master of ceremonies come on top. A well-known artist can cost more than the rest of the budget on their own: that is a call to make early.

5. Decoration and set design: 5 to 15%

From a few thousand euros for floral dressing and signage to several tens of thousands for a bespoke set. This is where a party takes on your brand's identity.

6. Staff, security and contingencies: 5 to 10%

Hosts and hostesses, cloakroom, security guards, photographer, insurance. Always keep a 5 to 10% buffer for the unexpected: an extra guest, an extra hour, rain that calls for a marquee.

7. Agency fees

An agency charges production fees, as a flat fee or a percentage. On the market, this is most often 10 to 20% of the budget. In return, it negotiates with suppliers and carries the coordination and the risk on the day. At Dare-Dare, every quote is itemised line by line: you know exactly what you are paying for.

5 ways to make your budget go further

  1. Pick the right date. A Thursday is often cheaper than a Friday, and January or February far cheaper than December, when Paris venues are snapped up.
  2. Go for a walking dinner rather than a seated one: less service, more mingling.
  3. Choose a venue that is already beautiful. A venue with character needs less decoration.
  4. Focus the budget on one big moment. One great surprise leaves more of a mark than a dozen average activities.
  5. Book early. Two to three months ahead, and four to six months for an end-of-year party.

Frequently asked questions

What is the budget per person for a corporate party in Paris?

As a rule, allow €150 to €250 excl. VAT per guest for a simple, well-executed party, €250 to €450 excl. VAT for a premium party with a venue full of character and real set design, and over €450 excl. VAT for a prestige event in an iconic venue. These ranges cover venue, catering, drinks, technical production, entertainment and staff.

What is the most expensive item in a corporate party budget?

Catering and drinks often account for 30 to 40% of the budget, followed by venue hire (20 to 30%). Technical production, set design and entertainment share the rest.

How much does an event agency charge for a corporate party?

An agency charges production fees, either as a flat fee or as a percentage of the budget. On the market, this is most often 10 to 20% of the total budget. At Dare-Dare, fees depend on the complexity of the project, and every quote is itemised line by line.

How far in advance should you book a corporate party in Paris?

Ideally 2 to 3 months, and 4 to 6 months for an end-of-year party in December, when Paris venues are most in demand. That said, a party can be produced in 3 to 4 weeks if the brief is clear.

A good budget isn't the biggest one: it is the one that puts the money where your guests will see it. Before asking for quotes, pin down three numbers: guest count, the level of party you're after and your maximum spend. Everything else is built around them.
